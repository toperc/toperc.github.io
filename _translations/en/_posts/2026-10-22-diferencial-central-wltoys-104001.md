After the [104001 review]({{ '/reviews/wltoys-104001/' | relative_url }}), the next step was to revisit a modification I had used in my other car: **adding a centre differential**. This documents the assembly; the electronics conversion comes later.

<!--more-->

## Parts for This Installation

I used an original 104001 differential, an LC Racing spur gear and two dogbones: **100mm at the rear and 70mm at the front** in the final assembly. I also reused drive cups and a bearing from the original centre transmission.

The new spur has different teeth from the original. The motor pinion must match it: touching teeth do not establish correct meshing. The links below are references from the description; check variants and dimensions before ordering.

## Preparing the Differential

I opened the differential and transferred the cup to the new spur, retaining the seal, washer and pin. After replacing the internal gear, I started all screws before tightening progressively in a cross pattern.

I checked rotation and added the bearing recovered from the original transmission. Keep the small parts accounted for throughout.

## Changing the Transmission Connections

I removed the top plate and spur covers, then replaced the original centre connections with the cups for the new dogbones. Some parts were very difficult to remove; I dismantled part of the steering to gain access.

When fitting the cups, I checked the shaft flats and screw seating. Before continuing I verified that turning each cup moved the wheels without abnormal resistance.

## The Top Plate Needs Clearance

The original plate rubbed against the new assembly. I relieved the interfering areas and tested movement before closing. The video offers no universal cutting dimensions: trial-fit the actual combination and check clearance.

I fitted the long dogbone at the rear and the short one at the front, then checked motor position and gear mesh.

## The Spur Remains Exposed

The original covers no longer fit this arrangement, leaving the spur exposed to stones and dirt that could damage its teeth. The modification has trade-offs.

Keep wiring clear too: neither motor wires nor the battery balance lead can approach the transmission. My car still had the original electronics at this stage.

## What Is Checked Here

I bench-checked differential operation and movement distribution between the axles. **I did not drive this configuration with the original motor and pinion.** I already intended to replace them; the meshing heard during the presentation was not ready for driving.
