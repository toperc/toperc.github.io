I bought this **used Wltoys 144001 with a supposed motor fault**. Before ordering parts, I wanted to establish the actual failure and inspect the rest of the car while dismantling it. The result is a two-part restoration with faults, stubborn screws and several upgrades while everything is open.

<!--more-->

## First, Find Out What Works

Steering responded, but the motor did not turn with the trigger. I checked the motor separately and found it worked; replacing the ESC eventually restored throttle response.

**Working steering alone does not identify the failed component.** Check power, connections, transmitter and ESC before concluding a diagnosis. The video directly tests the motor with a battery and loose wires; I am not presenting that as a beginner procedure because accidental contact can cause a short circuit. Use a suitable test setup and insulated connections.

Beyond the electrical fault, I found missing transmission parts, a rough bearing and front arms that would not drop freely. All mattered before driving.

## Dismantle Methodically

I started with wheels and shocks, separating screws, bushings and small parts. This car has short shocks in front and long ones at the rear. Labelling avoids swapping them.

Without shocks, arm movement is easier to inspect. The front resisted movement; cleaning and lubricating hinge pins and checking tightness clearly improved it. A stiff arm should not automatically be blamed on a bent pin.

The correct Phillips bit helps considerably. Even so, a differential screw stripped, and primary-shaft screws forced me to wait for a replacement. Allow for surprises when opening a used car.

## Recovering the Shocks

I dismantled, cleaned and checked shafts, seals, pistons and diaphragms. Refilling oil does not repair a scratched shaft or damaged seal.

I chose **400 cSt oil** for this restoration, rather than prescribing it for every 144001. I moved the piston slowly, allowed air to escape and checked travel before refitting springs.

One shock had too little travel after closing because it contained too much fluid. I corrected the fill and checked again. Clean and freshly refilled does not necessarily mean functioning correctly.

## Differentials and Transmission

At the front I fitted thicker cups with their seals and tried **10,000 cSt** silicone. That is not a promise of a leakproof differential: these parts require monitoring for leaks and wear.

One differential bound during assembly and had to be reopened and adjusted. Before final closure I checked gear and washer seating and correct rotation.

The metal-spur conversion stopped in part one because of primary-shaft screws. In part two I fitted the replacement shaft, spur and bearings. Metal was this project's choice, rather than a reason to replace every functioning plastic spur.

## Assemble and Check in Stages

Part two completes the car with the replacement ESC, new bearings and thicker drivetrain parts. I checked movement after each assembly, before other parts made access harder.

The recurring checks were:

1. Bearings must seat properly and covers close without force.
2. The drivetrain must turn freely, with both axles driving in the same direction.
3. Steering must move freely with bushings fitted and servo centred.
4. Shocks and arms must retain their travel after tightening.
5. Check tyres, glue and battery restraint before driving.

I also replaced missing battery supports with 3D-printed parts and a strap. No print file is published because it is not included in the source material.

## What the Finished Work Demonstrates

The second video ends with the car assembled, steering and motor working and better suspension behaviour on the bench. **It does not include an outdoor driving test**, so I draw no conclusions about speed, times or post-restoration durability.
