I bought my **Tamiya TT-02 used and already prepared**, intending to enter a category cup. This video explains why kit price does not necessarily represent the cost of a complete setup for your goal.

<!--more-->

## Building Is Part of the Hobby Too

Assembling parts, following the manual and understanding transmission and suspension are major TT-02 benefits. If you enjoy the process, it can matter as much as later driving.

For box-to-driving use, check the package carefully. Servo, radio, battery, paint and other items may be additional; electronics vary between kits.

## Budget Depends on Your Goal

I add up kit, paint, bearings, ball joints, servo and electronics in the video. Those are historical examples, rather than current prices or mandatory upgrades for enjoyment.

I was preparing for a specific category. That does not make a kit's brushed motor unusable or always require replacing it.

Before buying upgrades, decide whether you want learning, recreational driving or a competition with defined rules.

## What Mine Had

The used car already had bearings, replacement steering elements, a servo, RadioMaster MT12 and different electronics. I also found a clear difference in front/rear differential resistance.

This is my starting configuration, **not a universal setup or verified compliance for every competition**. The description lists equipment I use.

## Read the Rules Before Spending

When recording, I was waiting for season rules and discussing rumours. I do not present those as approved bans or current regulations.

Motor, ESC, tyres, transmission and body may have specific requirements. Consult the actual event rules before buying, especially when reusing someone else's prepared car.

## My View After Buying

I am happy with the car and learning opportunities. It is also easy to spend heavily without defining a goal. Buying used provided a complete setup, subject to inspecting condition and included equipment.
