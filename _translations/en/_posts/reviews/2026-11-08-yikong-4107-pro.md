Yes, I bought a crawler. If you know the channel, you know I enjoy speed and that crawling was never my favourite RC discipline. But the **Yikong 4107 Pro** has changed my mind: after driving it on rocks and at the beach, I have found a way to enjoy RC that I was not expecting.

The number of functions caught my attention, especially being able to operate them **from the transmitter**. After using the truck, I have also found things I would like to improve. This review covers both sides.

## Why I chose this crawler

I was not looking to replace my on-road cars. I wanted to try something I could take on a trail and drive at a different pace, perhaps during a walk in the hills. The Yikong appealed to me because of everything it offered in one truck.

It is a 1:10-scale 4WD crawler with a Patrol-style body. In the review, I show its brushless outrunner motor, two-speed gearbox, lighting system and the servos that operate its functions. Those mechanical features become much more interesting when you start testing what each control does.

The transmitter has eight channels. It lets me change gear, control the lights, operate the front and rear differential locks and activate tank-turn mode. That convenience was one of my main reasons for buying it.

## Two differentials: one at the front and one at the rear

The truck has **a differential in each axle**. With an open differential, the two wheels on that axle can turn at different speeds, which helps when cornering. Locking it forces both wheels to turn together; that can help when one has much less grip than the other.

On the Yikong, a micro servo pulls a cable to release the differential mechanism. When it stops pulling, a spring returns it to the locked position. In the video, I show the mechanism and the transmitter indicators: a lit indicator means the differential is locked; an unlit one means it is unlocked.

Being able to control each axle from the transmitter is useful while driving. You can experiment in the same section of terrain and see how the truck responds.

![Yikong electronics and mechanisms with the body open]({{ '/img/reviews/yikong-4107-pro/electronica.webp' | relative_url }})

## What tank-turn mode does

Another function I demonstrate is **tank-turn mode**, which helps tighten a turn by acting on the inside rear wheel. The system needs the rear differential to be open so the wheels on that axle can behave differently.

There is a useful detail in the controls: if both differentials are locked and I activate this function, the transmitter releases the rear differential lock. I do not have to do that as a separate step.

I still do not know how much I will use it on my routes. For now, I like having the option and understanding how it works before making it a habit.

## 2S or 3S: which battery I would start with

The truck accepts **2S and 3S LiPo batteries**. For my style of driving, moving slowly and looking for grip, I would start with 2S. Extra speed is not my main interest here; I care more about throttle feel and controlling the truck's movement.

In the review, I mention a roughly 5000 mAh 2S pack as an option for a good session of driving, but I have not measured runtime in a way that would justify promising a particular duration. Terrain, driving style and the battery all affect the result.

3S remains a compatible option if you want a different response. Before buying a pack, also check its dimensions, connector and fit in the battery tray.

## What surprised me most about driving it

I am used to wanting my cars to go a little faster. With the crawler, something different happened: I started enjoying **choosing a line, finding a wheel placement and clearing an obstacle at low speed**.

It helped me switch off. I did not need to push the truck to its limits all the time; if a line did not work, I could try another angle. That feeling is what I have enjoyed most about this first experience.

I also like the scale realism. Accessories, body details and a good camera angle can make you look twice before realising you are watching an RC vehicle. The final part of the video includes a driving session so you can see it moving and form your own impression.

## The areas I want to improve

### Body and bumper rubbing

When the suspension articulates, the tyres can touch the wheel arches and front bumper. On some obstacles, that rubbing held me back quite noticeably.

In the video, I explain how to move the bumper slightly using its mounts. **This reduces the rubbing but does not eliminate it.** It is worth keeping in mind when judging the truck in stock form.

### Ride height and shock mounting positions

The shock towers offer several mounting positions. Changing the position can gain some height and reduce contact with the body. In the review, I show the front and rear options and describe the adjustment that helped me.

I do not present this as a permanent fix either: I want to keep testing which combination works best for my driving before considering the issue settled.

![Yikong wheel, body and suspension during the adjustment demonstration]({{ '/img/reviews/yikong-4107-pro/suspension.webp' | relative_url }})

### The servo saver and steering feel

With a wheel pressed against a surface that offers resistance, I have noticed that the servo saver can give before the wheel turns as expected. The servo moves, but some of that movement is absorbed by the mechanism, making the steering feel less precise.

This is one area I want to investigate. In the review, I discuss the possibility of modifying it or replacing the link, but **I have not tested that improvement yet**. The truck is enjoyable in stock form; it is simply something I would like to refine.

## Do I regret buying it?

Not so far. The Yikong 4107 Pro has introduced me to a side of RC I thought I would not enjoy. Its transmitter-controlled functions are convenient, and I have had a great time finding lines through the rocks.

There is room for improvement in tyre clearance and steering feel. I prefer to discuss that alongside the positives so the review reflects the truck I am actually using. I already have ideas and accessories for future videos, but this assessment covers my first stage with it.
