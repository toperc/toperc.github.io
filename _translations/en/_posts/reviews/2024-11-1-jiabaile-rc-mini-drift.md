The **Jiabaila 1:43** has a simple proposition: indoor drifting with a car that fits in your palm. I had owned earlier versions with and without a gyro; this time I tried one of the new bodies. It remains a fun little toy, especially with another car for friendly racing.

<!--more-->

## What Came with the Reviewed Car

The box contained the car, transmitter, cones, screwdriver and a USB-C charging cable. Two tyre types were supplied: fitted drift tyres and another rubber set with more grip.

This was the **gyro version**, with an AE86-style body and manually lowered pop-up headlights. Different bodies and versions exist; check exactly which you are buying, particularly if you want a gyro.

## The Gyro Helps, but You Still Need Practice

The transmitter adjusts gyro strength. The video shows steering corrections without me turning the wheel; the assistance is welcome initially because spinning is easy.

There are three speed levels and a lighting control. **Throttle and steering are proportional**, allowing modulation rather than only full-on or off.

On the tested car all four wheels turn together, without differentials distributing rotation. It has a brushed motor and very simple suspension, appropriate to a small drift toy with basic mechanics.

## How It Behaves at Home

Practice is necessary. Adjusting the gyro helps but does not make perfect sideways driving immediate. I would prefer more steering angle for drift control.

Surface matters too. On parquet, **wheel noise is quite noticeable**, something to consider for living-room use.

I would reserve it for indoors and smooth surfaces, rather than streets or rough ground. Setting up a small cone course and sharing it with someone is where I enjoy it most.

## Charging and Runtime

Charging uses USB-C. I mention being surprised by charging speed and battery life compared with earlier cars I had owned.

That is a user impression: **this test contains no timed charging or runtime measurement** and does not confirm battery capacity. I therefore give no minutes or mAh figure for this car.

## The Fault I Had to Repair

One headlight arrived with a loose wire. I opened the car, soldered the LED wire and closed the body again. Access needs few screws, but easy access does not excuse a fault on the received car.

Wheel changes are possible, although removing and fitting them is harder than expected because the parts are so small.

## Would I Buy It Again?

Yes, for indoor drifting and fun. I particularly like the size, two tyre sets and adjustable gyro. The trade-offs are steering with room for improvement, noise on some surfaces and assembly quality that required a repair on mine.

If this type of mini RC appeals, **check that you choose the gyro version**. That is what I tested and what seems more forgiving for learning to drift.
