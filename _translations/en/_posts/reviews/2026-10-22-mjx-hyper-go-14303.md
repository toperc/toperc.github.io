The **MJX Hyper Go 14303** appeals for asphalt driving and playing with slides. On dirt I found a limitation that spoiled the experience: stones entered the steering mechanism, repeatedly forcing me to stop and remove them.

<!--more-->

## What I Liked

This compact model has a Citroën-inspired rally body, brushless motor, separate ESC and receiver, gyro and an internal cover protecting some electronics.

I like the lighting: mine activates indicators when turning and allows the lights to be switched off from the transmitter. The drawback is unplugging its wire to remove the body completely.

Initial construction felt solid. Oil suspension felt reasonable, although fixed arms offer less adjustment than adjustable links.

## Box Contents Depend on the Package

My selected offer included 2S and 3S batteries and wheel sets for different uses. The material shown also includes a small ESC fan.

Do not assume every current offer contains the same items. Check electronics version, batteries and accessories, and verify compatibility before changing voltage.

Drift wheels do not make it my first drift choice: the steering angle did not convince me for that discipline.

## Throttle Response I Could Not Change

Throttle started progressively then surged, as though following a pronounced curve. Changing transmitter did not remove it.

I could not program my ESC to eliminate that behaviour. That is what I checked, without assigning an internal cause I neither dismantled nor measured.

Reduced-power mode may help familiarisation. The gyro also assists, but cannot correct mechanically jammed steering.

## Stones Were the Dirt Problem

During testing, the servo/steering layout allowed stone accumulation. The internal cover did not prevent entry there. Turning became restricted in one or both directions, requiring stops to clear it.

I suggested a possible 3D-printed layout change, but **did not develop or test that solution**. It is not an available verified repair.

For that stony ground I would prefer a car requiring fewer stops. This limitation matters more to me than rally styling.

## Maintenance Access Matters Too

Differential access requires removing numerous parts. Phillips screws and difficult fasteners complicate work, especially with a poor bit or a screw starting to strip.

Repairability is valuable, but I claim neither quick maintenance nor current availability of every spare. Check the exact part and model variant.

## My Use-Dependent Conclusion

I find it entertaining on asphalt, with appealing details and solid-feeling construction on the tested car. I would not repeat the stony outings without resolving steering first.
