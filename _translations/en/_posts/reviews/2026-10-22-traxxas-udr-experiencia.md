The **Traxxas UDR** won me over visually: its suspension and movement were particularly appealing. I bought one used and dismantled, repaired it and drove it. Even so, I eventually sold it.

<!--more-->

## A Purchase That Started on the Workbench

The car needed transmission repair. Its previous owner explained that he had been unable to reassemble it.

I ordered parts, serviced the differentials and adapted the power connection to my equipment. **My experience does not begin with a brand-new, boxed UDR**.

## Access Was Not Convenient for Me

The amount of dismantling to reach certain areas weighed on me. It felt like considerable work to service small transmission parts.

I give neither a measured repair time nor a list of faults every car will suffer. This explains why its maintenance felt less rewarding than that of other cars I used.

## Its Handling Was Not What I Wanted

Repaired, it worked and I enjoyed watching it move. At the circuit, however, I struggled with corners and inertia and could not find the feel I knew from buggies.

The locked rear in my configuration also surprised me. I wanted conventional differential tuning and did not want further purchases to change its character.

That is a preference, rather than a conclusion that UDR architecture is faulty because it behaves differently.

## Shared Driving Mattered Too

I increasingly enjoy meeting enthusiasts and driving similar-handling cars together. With the UDR I struggled to find that rhythm and did not want the required learning commitment.

I also could not obtain the throttle-based airborne corrections I wanted. I describe attempts with 4S and 6S without an instrumental configuration comparison.

## Why I Sold It

I still liked its looks but preferred space and budget for cars I used more. I do not regret selling: it was about my use, rather than nobody being able to enjoy the model.
