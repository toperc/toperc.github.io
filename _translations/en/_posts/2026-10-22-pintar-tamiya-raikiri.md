This was my second airbrushed body and my first attempt at a more complex pattern. **Vallejo supplied the airbrush and some paints**; I added other colours and started work on a Tamiya Raikiri.

<!--more-->

## Preparing the Body and Design

I cleaned and dried the body, then drew outside the pattern I wanted to see: pink and blue sections separated by white squares.

A ruler helped maintain the lines, and reference colours identified each section. Paint goes inside; the outside drawing guides the internal masking.

## Choosing the Masking Method

I had tried brush-applied liquid mask but found it awkward in earlier attempts. For this design I used tape and prepared the areas patiently.

That was my choice for this job, not a complete comparison of every method. Where masking was imperfect, the result shows small colour bleeds.

## Paint Order Created Extra Work

I began with the white squares, then realised the sequence was poorly planned. I continued with the other sections and finished with white backing inside to bring out the colours.

That improvisation is not a universal layer recipe. Plan what will be visible through each area and follow the paint instructions for your body's material.

The recording also shows a final black backing. Its visual effect depends on previous layers; adding it does not guarantee finish durability.

## Unmasking and Seeing the Result

I removed tape and exterior film, applied the stickers I wanted and fitted the wing. Revealing the clean exterior design after all that masking was the most rewarding part.

Small mistakes remained, but I was much happier than expected. This is not a professional painting class: it shows what I achieved with limited experience and what I would improve next time.
